4 The Lie algebra

4.1 The 5-grading of the Lie algebra

The Lie algebra \(L\) over any conic algebra \(C\) has a direct sum decomposition \(\bigoplus_{i=-2}^2 L_i\). As usual in GAP, the sum and Lie bracket of two elements of \(L\) can be computed as lie1 + lie2 and lie1 * lie2, respectively. Further, the scalar multiplication \(k \times L \to L\), where \(k\) is the base ring of \(C\), can also be computed using *.

4.1-1 LiePart
‣ LiePart( lie, p )( operation )

Returns the projection of lie to \(L_p\) where -2 <= p <= 2. This is an element of \(k\) if \(p = \pm 2\), and element of \(B\) if \(p = \pm 1\) and an element of \(L_0\) if \(p=0\).

4.1-2 LieZero and L0Zero
‣ LieZero( C )( operation )
‣ L0Zero( C )( operation )

Returns the zero element of \(L\) or \(L_0\), respectively.

4.2 The Brown algebra and its elements

We refer to the \(k\)-module \(B = (k, J, J', k)\) as the Brown algebra because, if \(2\) and \(3\) are invertible in \(k\), then it has the structure of a structurable algebra that is known under this name. It parametrises the \(1\)- and \((-1)\)-parts of the Lie algebra that is constructed in [DW26].

Note that while elements of \(B\) are internally represented and also printed as lists [t, cub1, cub2, s], they are not lists in the GAP sense. Thus for an element brown of \(B\), we have IsList(brown) = false and brown[1] is not a well-defined expression. Further, we also distinguish between the element [t, cub1, cub2, s] of the Brown algebra and the corresponding elements [t, cub1, cub2, s]_+ and [t, cub1, cub2, s]_- of \(L_1\) and \(L_{-1}\), respectively.

4.2-1 BrownEl
‣ BrownEl( t, cub1, cub2, s )( operation )

Returns the element [ t, cub1, cub2, s ] of \(B\).

4.2-2 BrownElTuple

Returns the GAP list [ t, cub1, cub2, s ] underlying brown.

4.2-3 BrownElPart

4.2-4 BrownElComPart

4.2-5 BrownElCubicPart

Returns BrownElPart(brown, 2) for p=1 and BrownElPart(brown, 3) for p=2.

4.4 The parts \(L_{-1}\) and \(L_1\)

The \(k\)-submodules \(L_{-1}\) and \(L_1\) are isomorphic to the Brown algebra \(B\). However, as explained previously, we strictly distinguish between elements of \(B\), \(L_{-1}\) and \(L_1\).

4.5 The Lie subalgebra \(L_0\)

The subalgebra \(L_0\) of \(L\) is itself the direct sum of three components \(L_{0,-1}, Z, L_{0,1}\). We internally distinguish between elements of \(L_0\) and their embedding into \(L\).

The \(k\)-submodules \(L_{0,-1}\) and \(L_{0,1}\) are isomorphic to the cubic Jordan matrix algebra \(J\). Again, we strictly distinguish between elements of \(J\), \(L_{0,-1}\) and \(L_{0,1}\). For an element cub of \(J\), the corresponding elements of \(L_{0,-1}\) and \(L_{0,1}\) are printed as ad_{cub}^- and ad_{cub}^+, respectively. While we internally distinguish between elements of \(L_{0,\pm 1}\) and their embeddings into \(L\), they are printed in the same way.

We refer to the \(k\)-module \(\langle \mathbf{d}_{a,b} \mid a \in J, b \in J' \rangle\) (which is unnamed in [DW26]) as DD in (the documentation of) this GAP package. The \(k\)-module \(Z\) is the (not necessarily direct) sum of DD with \(k \xi + k\zeta\). Again, we internally distinguish between elements of DD and their embedding into \(L_0\) and \(L\).

4.6 Root homomorphisms in the Lie algebra

We represent a root in \(G_2\) (respectively, \(F_4\)) as a list with two (respectively, four) elements, each of which is an integer in the range [-2, -1, 0, 1, 2]. This representation corresponds precisely to Figures 1 and 3 in [DW26].

4.7 Automorphisms of the Lie algebra

The \(F_4\)-graded group constructed in [DW26] is a group of automorphisms of \(L\). This package provides the internal structure LieEndo to perform computations with endomorphisms of \(L\).

For an element f of LieEndo and lie in \(L\), the image of lie under f can be computed as f(lie). For two elements f and g of LieEndo, f+g and f*g are the elements of LieEndo that map lie to f(lie)+g(lie) and f(g(lie)), respectively.

4.7-1 LieEndoOne
‣ LieEndoOne( global variable )

The identity element in the automorphism group of the Lie algebra over a conic algebra. It is the same for any conic algebra.

4.7-2 GrpRootHomF4
‣ GrpRootHomF4( F4Root, z )( operation )

Returns the automorphism \(\theta_\alpha(a)\) of \(L\) as defined in 11.2 and 11.8 in [DW26]. Thus z must be in \(k\) if F4Root is long and in \(C\) otherwise.

4.7-3 GrpRootHomF4
‣ GrpRootHomF4( F4Root, z )( operation )

Returns the automorphism \(\theta_\alpha(a)\) of \(L\) as defined in 11.2 and 11.8 in [DW26]. Thus z must be in \(k\) if F4Root is long and in \(C\) otherwise.

4.7-4 GrpStandardWeylF4
‣ GrpStandardWeylF4( F4Root )( operation )
